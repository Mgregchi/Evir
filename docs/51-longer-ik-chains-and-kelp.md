# Longer IK chains and Kelp

21 October 2026. The [Rive IK and bones guides](../research/software-closure/sources.json) document chain length, bend direction, strength, independent targets and weighted handles. Reading the pinned IKConstraint, Bone, Constraint and TransformComponent implementations established the update order: each solving step explicitly composes its pair and tip, while other FK world matrices remain snapshots until their step. Recomputing the entire hierarchy between steps produces a different long-chain pose.

Evir now supports one to eight bones per IK constraint with unit positive scales, an independent target and disjoint constrained chains. The existing one/two-bone solver remains in place; longer chains follow the researched sequential solve. Strength mixes solved local rotations with the original FK pose after the complete solve. A zero-strength constraint preserves FK exactly. Coincident, aligned and unreachable targets remain finite and have separate native comparisons. Nonunit scales, overlapping chains and animated constraint strength are still outside this contract.

Select a chain's last bone and open **Properties → Inverse kinematics…**. Choose the independent target, chain length, bend direction and strength. Missing ancestors reject without changing the project. Apply is one Undo step. Move the target to pose the chain; animate its X/Y for a looping motion. Source save/recovery, native export and supported editable import retain the constraint. The local command schema exposes `set_ik` with a complete constraint or `null` to remove it. It also exposes `bind_skin` and `set_skin_weights`; scope, ancestor locks, validation and atomic history apply.

**Kelp** is original editable vector artwork: a mint sea creature with three curved resting tendrils and a waving gradient-stroked tendril. Four bones deform its controls and follow an independently animated target. Open Kelp from Examples, play **A little wave**, then inspect **wave target**, **wave bone 4** and **hello tendril** in Studio. Its source, `.riv` and actual Canvas poster are generated from [kelp-scene.mjs](../apps/web/kelp-scene.mjs). No external imagery or altered brand artwork is included.

[Unit tests](../tests/runtime/ik-chains.test.mjs) cover counts 3/4/8, strength, direction, source immutability, preserved lengths, zero-strength FK, invalid chains and edge targets. [Pixel comparisons](../research/results/software-closure/compounds/) cover 90 animated poses per official Canvas/WebGL2 backend, plus 12 edge cases and Kelp's five animation samples per backend. The first eight-bone half-strength experiment failed the unchanged image threshold; comparing actual native bone matrices identified the update-order difference before the implementation was corrected. [The matrix oracle](../tools/validate-ik-poses.cjs) additionally compares all six world-matrix values for every bone across those animated cases, with a 0.01-source-unit absolute tolerance. No pixel threshold was relaxed.

[Responsive Studio evidence](../research/results/software-closure/ik-chains/) records count/bend/strength editing, rejection, Undo/Redo, preview, actual export, recovery and automated accessibility. This remains cloud automation, not physical-device or human testing. Rive's MIT notice is distributed as `@evir/runtime/LICENSE.rive-ik` alongside the adapted solve.
